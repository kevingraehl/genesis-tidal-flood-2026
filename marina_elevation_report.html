What remains uncertain

Water can arrive another way

Marina Green, Crissy Marsh, harbor edges and other lows can flood first. Rain that cannot drain against a high Bay, blocked inlets, sewer backup, groundwater and local wave runup are outside this terrain-only calculation.

Does the 11 ft stress test mean the Marina is fine?

It is reassuring for the requested Beach Street block—but not a guarantee. At 11 ft NAVD88, the model still does not connect tidal water to Beach & Pierce. It begins connecting to the Pierce reference near 14.01 ft. Local garages can flood earlier through rain, waves, a low threshold, or drainage backup.

Can water come backward through drains?

Yes, under some conditions. Most of San Francisco uses a combined sewer. When capacity is exceeded, water can emerge through catch basins or manholes, and a building lateral can back up through low fixtures or floor drains. A maintained property backwater valve can close against reverse flow; no public source reviewed identifies one master neighborhood valve for this Marina block.

Other San Francisco areas at risk

The City identifies Mission Bay, Islais Creek, Hunters Point, Candlestick Point, the Financial District/Embarcadero, the Marina, Treasure Island, and SFO as particularly susceptible to increasing coastal-flood risk. This map only analyzes the Marina/Crissy study area.

What did 2007 climate projections say?

IPCC’s 2007 scenarios projected roughly 0.18–0.59 m (0.6–1.9 ft) of global rise by 2090–2099 versus 1980–1999. A rough straight-line slice to 2027 is about 0.2–0.7 ft, shown only for context. It is not a local 2027 tide prediction and should not be added again to today’s measured or forecast water levels.

How water can arrive

Four different flood mechanisms

Direct tidal flooding

Bay water crosses a shoreline low point and follows connected terrain. The NWS forecast reaches low shoreline and marsh areas, but the DEM screen does not connect 8.66 ft NAVD88 to the requested Beach Street intersections.

Wave overtopping

Breaking waves and runup can throw water above the still-water level. NWS also issued a High Surf Warning; this simulator does not model those waves.

Rain and blocked inlets

Water can pond where catch basins are blocked or rain arrives faster than the combined sewer can accept it. That may happen above the tide line.

Sewer or drain backup

Most of San Francisco uses a combined sewer. A property backwater valve can automatically close against reverse flow, but only a plumber or SFPUC can verify the actual lateral, fixtures, and valve condition.

Before Tuesday morning

Reasonable protective action

  1. Clear and photograph nearby catch basins. Report blocked drains through 311; do not remove a grate or enter moving water.
  2. Move vulnerable items up. Keep cardboard, batteries, tools, and electronics off garage floors in known ponding locations.
  3. Use a real temporary barrier where a threshold is low. Sandbags or a purpose-built flood barrier can redirect shallow surface water. Do not block a public drain.
  4. Stage a pump and hose. A low-profile automatic pump can remove garage-floor water while power is available; a fused 12 V bilge pump and deep-cycle battery can provide backup.
  5. Use tape only as a shallow-seepage helper. Butyl or Flex Tape can supplement plastic and sandbags on a clean, dry threshold, but cannot resist deep water or replace a flood barrier.
  6. Check backwater protection professionally. A building backwater valve can protect a low fixture or floor drain from reverse sewer flow, but it must be correctly located and maintained.

Data and limits

Methodology

The analysis uses the USGS CA_SanFrancisco_B23 0.25 m hydro-flattened bare-earth DEM, collected April 20, 2023 in EPSG:7131 with elevations in NAVD88 (Geoid18). The project reports 1.63 cm NVA RMSEz and 3.19 cm tested NVA at 95% confidence; local sampling uncertainty is larger where curbs, interpolation, vegetation, construction, or geocoding affect a point.

NOAA station 9414290 lists NAVD88 at 5.92 ft and MLLW at 5.98 ft above station datum, so NAVD88 = MLLW + 0.06 ft. The 7.029 ft astronomical tide becomes 7.089 ft NAVD88; the NWS 8.6 ft MLLW surge forecast becomes 8.66 ft NAVD88.
